Module 01
Calculations without slips
01

Every calculation on the exam reduces to five relationships. Write the units at every step; most lost points were unit or decimal slips, not wrong formulas.

Amplitude (µV) = sensitivity (µV/mm) × deflection (mm)
A larger µV/mm number means LOWER sensitivity: smaller waves.
speed (mm/s) = page width (mm) ÷ page time (s)
Never assume 30 mm/s: compute it from the page every time.
duration (s) = width (mm) ÷ speed  ·  f (Hz) = 1 ÷ period
Spike < 70 ms · sharp wave 70–200 ms.
LFF = 1 ÷ (2π · TC)  ·  TC = 1 ÷ (2π · LFF)
TC is the time to decay to 37% on a square-wave calibration.
samples = duration (s) × sampling rate · Nyquist = rate ÷ 2
A filter passes ~70% (−3 dB) at its own cutoff.

Time-constant anchors: memorise these three

Time constantLFF
1 s0.16 Hz
0.3 s0.53 Hz
0.1 s1.6 Hz

Shortcut: LFF ≈ 0.16 ÷ TC. Halve the TC and the LFF doubles.

Your traps on Mock 1
Decimal slip: TC 0.1 s gives 1.6 Hz, not 0.16 Hz. Sanity-check against the anchors.
Seconds read as hertz: an LFF of 0.5 Hz is not a 0.5-s time constant; it is 0.32 s.
Assumed speed: a 20-s page across 300 mm runs at 15 mm/s, not 30.
Module 01 · continued

Worked examples · cover the solution, then check

Page width
A 20-s review page is 300 mm wide. LPDs recur every 24 mm. What is their frequency?
  1. Speed = 300 mm ÷ 20 s = 15 mm/s
  2. Period = 24 mm ÷ 15 mm/s = 1.6 s
  3. f = 1 ÷ 1.6 s = 0.63 Hz

At an assumed 30 mm/s you would report 1.25 Hz, double the truth.

Duration
On a 10-s page 250 mm wide, a transient is 3 mm wide at its base. Spike or sharp wave?
  1. Speed = 250 ÷ 10 = 25 mm/s
  2. Duration = 3 ÷ 25 = 0.12 s = 120 ms
  3. 70–200 ms → sharp wave

Assuming 30 mm/s gives 100 ms. Same class here, but not always.

Time constant
A square-wave calibration decays to 37% after 0.2 s. What LFF is applied?
  1. Decay to 37% → TC = 0.2 s
  2. LFF = 1 ÷ (2π × 0.2) = 1 ÷ 1.257
  3. = 0.8 Hz (check: 0.16 ÷ 0.2 = 0.8 ✓)
Sampling
A 30-ms spike is digitised at 200 samples/s. How many samples describe it?
  1. Convert: 30 ms = 0.030 s
  2. 0.030 s × 200 /s = 6 samples
  3. Few samples can clip the true peak, so the amplitude may be underestimated

Module 01 · continued

Gain, sensitivity and filters

Calibration error
50 µV at 5 µV/mm should give 10 mm. One channel deflects 12 mm.
Error = (12 − 10) ÷ 10 (expected) = 20% high
Divide by the expected value, not the observed one.
Sensitivity
A wave is 7 mm tall at 10 µV/mm. Sensitivity becomes 7 µV/mm.
Amplitude = 10 × 7 = 70 µV; new height = 70 ÷ 7 = 10 mm
The voltage never changes; only its drawing does.
70% rule
HFF 35 Hz. A 35-Hz component is truly 60 µV.
At the cutoff ≈ 70% passes: 0.7 × 60 = ≈ 42 µV
Not 50% (that is −6 dB), not unchanged, not removed.
The 20-second habit
Before any page-based calculation, write the speed in the margin: width ÷ time = mm/s. It takes 5 seconds and removes the most common Mock 1 error.
Sanity checks
Alpha is 8–13 Hz; LPDs are usually 0.5–2.5 Hz; spikes are tens of ms. If an answer falls far outside the expected range, re-check the units before choosing it.

Module 02
Polarity you can trust
02

Three of your misses were the same mistake: a sign. Use one rule, applied the same way every time, and polarity questions become arithmetic.

Channel = Input 1 − Input 2
Negative result → pen goes UP. Positive result → pen goes DOWN.
Pens point TOWARD each other → negative maximum
Pens point AWAY from each other → positive maximum, at the shared electrode.
No reversal + biggest at the end of a chain
→ maximum at the end electrode (end-of-chain). Confirm with another montage.
Your traps on Mock 1
Adding instead of subtracting: −60 − (−100) = +40, not −160.
Average reference sign: one electrode at −380 µV (19 in the average) pulls the average to −20 µV, so every quiet electrode deflects down by 20 µV.
Eye dipole: the cornea is positive. Wherever the eyes turn, the electrodes there go positive.

Module 02 · continued

References: when the reference itself is active

Recognise it: every channel that shares one reference shows the same deflection, even electrodes far from the discharge; the true maximum can look small or inverted.

Average reference arithmetic
19-electrode average. A pop drives O2 to +190 µV; all others are 0 µV. What do Cz-Avg and O2-Avg show?
  1. Average = +190 ÷ 19 = +10 µV
  2. Cz − Avg = 0 − 10 = −10 → Cz up 10 µV
  3. O2 − Avg = 190 − 10 = +180 → O2 down 180 µV

Choosing a reference for bilateral temporal spikes involving both ears

StateBest choiceWhy the others fail
AwakeCz referenceBoth ears carry spikes; Fpz is full of eye artifact. Cz is far from the temporal lobes and quiet while awake.
AsleepCommon averageCz now carries vertex waves and K-complexes; the average dilutes any single event across all electrodes.
Any stateBipolar chainsReference-free: use them to confirm localisation with phase reversals.
Remember the trade-off
No reference is "true". Pick the one farthest from the activity you are studying, and confirm in a second montage.

Module 03
ACNS 2021: classify by the numbers
03

Each critical-care term is decided by two numbers: frequency (count discharges ÷ seconds) and duration. Work them out first, then read the table.

TermCriteriaHow to recognise it fast
Electrographic seizureEpileptiform discharges averaging > 2.5 Hz for ≥ 10 s (> 25 in 10 s), or any pattern with definite evolution lasting ≥ 10 s9 discharges in 3 s = 3 Hz; held for 15 s → seizure, even without evolution
Electrographic status (ESE)Seizure lasting ≥ 10 continuous minutes, or seizures totalling ≥ 20% of any 60-minute period (≥ 12 min)13 min of seizures within one hour → ESE, even if none lasted 10 min
BIRDsRhythmic activity > 4 Hz, at least 6 waves, lasting 0.5 to < 10 s. Definite: evolution or resembles the patient's seizures. Possible: sharply contoured without evolution.7 s of 5 → 7 Hz speeding up = definite BIRDs (too short for a seizure)
Ictal-interictal continuum (IIC)PDs or SW averaging > 1 and ≤ 2.5 Hz for ≥ 10 s; or PDs/SW 0.5–1 Hz with a plus modifier or fluctuation; or LRDA > 1 Hz with a plus modifier or fluctuationGPDs at 1.5 Hz with triphasic morphology in liver failure → IIC; morphology and cause do not exclude it
LPDs / GPDs / LRDA / GRDAMain terms: localisation (G, L, BI, UI, Mf) + pattern (PD, RDA, SW); plus modifiers +F (fast activity), +R (PDs only), +S (RDA only)Asymmetric but synchronous PDs count as lateralized

Decision path

Your traps on Mock 1
"Seizures must evolve." No: a steady > 2.5 Hz discharge for ≥ 10 s already qualifies.
"Triphasic waves are metabolic, not IIC." In ACNS 2021 they are GPDs with triphasic morphology, classified by frequency like any other PD.
Technologist's role
Measure frequency and duration, test and document responsiveness, and notify the physician promptly; the physician makes the clinical diagnosis.
Module 04
Emergencies in the EEG lab
04

Emergency questions reward the first correct action. Learn each sequence as a fixed script (based on current AHA / Red Cross guidance; always follow your facility's protocol).

Choking infant (< 1 year), responsive

  1. 5 back blows, infant face-down along your forearm, head lower than chest
  2. 5 chest thrusts, face-up, heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone (AHA 2025 no longer prefers the two-finger technique)
  3. Repeat until the object comes out or the infant becomes unresponsive → start CPR and call for help

No abdominal thrusts in infants; no blind finger sweeps.

Cardiac arrest with EEG electrodes on

  1. Keep CPR going while the AED pads go on; do not pause for electrodes
  2. A pad must not sit over an electrode or patch: peel off the chest ECG electrode where the pad belongs
  3. Scalp electrodes stay on; they do not interfere with pad placement

Sedated child: snoring stops, SpO2 falls

  1. Effort without airflow means obstruction: open the airway with head tilt–chin lift or jaw thrust
  2. Call for help at the same time: summon the sedation nurse/rapid response team
  3. Oxygen helps only once the airway is open

Generalized convulsion during the EEG

  1. Time it and keep recording; protect the head, clear the area
  2. Nothing in the mouth; do not restrain the limbs
  3. Afterwards, place the patient on their side (recovery position) and check the airway
  4. Alert the nurse/physician at onset (per protocol); it is a medical emergency (status epilepticus) if it lasts ≥ 5 minutes, recurs without recovery, or the patient is injured or not breathing normally
Your traps on Mock 1
Choosing a reasonable but later step first (for example oxygen before opening the airway, or waiting before calling for help). On emergency questions ask: "What keeps the patient alive in the next 30 seconds?"
